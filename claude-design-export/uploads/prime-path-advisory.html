FAQ

Common questions, answered.

If yours isn't here, raise it on the strategy call — we'd rather talk through specifics than generalities.

Why do high-income W-2 earners overpay so consistently?

Because no one is paid to find the strategies that would prevent it. CPAs are trained for compliance — accurate filing of what you've already done. They're not retained or compensated to redesign your position. Our entire engagement begins where theirs ends.

How is your firm different from a CPA practice?

A CPA prepares your return. We engineer the position your return reports. The two functions are complementary but distinct. Many clients keep their CPA for filing and engage us for strategy; others move both functions under our roof. Either model works.

I'm a W-2 employee. Are these strategies actually available to me?

Yes. The widespread belief that W-2 earners have no tax-saving optionality is one of the most expensive misconceptions in personal finance. The legal vehicles available — investment structures, secondary income architecture, qualified plans, real estate strategies — are precisely the same ones used by the wealthy. They simply aren't marketed to you.

How much can I expect to save in the first year?

Documented engagements typically yield $150,000–$350,000 in first-year savings, with the figure compounding as additional strategies are layered in years two and three. Your specific outcome depends on income composition and current optimization, both of which we quantify in the diagnostic phase before any retainer is signed.

What happens under audit?

Every strategy we implement is documented to withstand examination. Our active client audit rate is 0.01% — substantially below the IRS baseline. In the rare case of inquiry, we represent and defend the position at no additional cost. Documentation isn't an afterthought; it's the deliverable.

How does the engagement begin?

With a complimentary strategy call. We review your current position, surface the largest opportunities, and quantify the savings on the table. If we're a fit, we proceed to a formal diagnostic. If not, you leave with a clearer picture than you arrived with — and no obligation either way.
